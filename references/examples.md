# Fictional Examples

All names, situations, and messages below were created for this public skill. They are not actual correspondence or records of sent emails.

## Routine request: workshop materials

### Initial example

Hi Alex,

Thank you very much for sending across the agenda. We have looked at it and it seems good. We are still discussing internally who will attend the workshop and will let you know when we know. Can you send the slides before the workshop so everyone can look at them?

Regards,
Sam

### Revised example

Hi Alex,

Thanks for sharing the agenda. It works for us.

We are confirming the attendees and will share the list once ready. In the meantime, could you send the slides so the team can review them before the workshop?

Regards,
Sam

### What changed

The revision separates acknowledgement, update, and request; removes repetition; and preserves the tentative status of the attendee list without inventing a deadline.

## Proposal: staged review of a website draft

### Facts supplied for this fictional scenario

The navigation is approved. Product descriptions are still under review. The sender wants design work to proceed while copy review continues. Neither a final delivery date nor publication approval has been agreed.

### Draft

Hi Jordan,

Thanks. We share the same understanding that the navigation is approved. To keep the work moving, we propose the following:

(1) Design review

Please proceed with the page layouts using the current text as a placeholder. We will share the revised product descriptions once our review is complete.

(2) Publication

We propose keeping the pages in draft until both the layouts and final text are approved. Please let us know if this approach affects your schedule.

Regards,
Sam

### Why this structure works

The two headings separate what can proceed from what still needs agreement. The draft presents publication arrangements as a proposal and asks about scheduling without inventing a delivery commitment.

## Review boundary

Changing `We expect to complete the review on Friday` to `We will complete the review by Friday` creates a firmer commitment. Retain the original degree of certainty or flag the change for the user.
