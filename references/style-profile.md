# External Business Email Style

These are reusable defaults, not a record of a specific person's correspondence.

## Voice and structure

- Prefer the shortest version that preserves the commercial objective and necessary legal meaning.
- Be courteous and cooperative without ceremonial formality, inflated warmth, or sales language.
- Preserve the author's clear wording; polish only where it improves meaning, naturalness, or concision.
- Give each paragraph one purpose. A routine sequence is greeting, brief acknowledgement, factual update, practical request, and short sign-off; omit steps that add nothing.
- For complex matters, establish common ground, explain the relevant facts in a useful order, present the proposal, acknowledge implementation needs, and invite a response.
- Use active phrasing such as `we propose`, `we are currently`, `we are prepared to`, and `please let us know` when the facts and intended level of commitment support them.

## Useful wording

Use these selectively, without turning every email into a template:

- `Thanks for reaching out.`
- `We are currently reviewing ... and will share ... once confirmed.`
- `In the meantime, to avoid delaying ...`
- `We share the same understanding regarding ...`
- `The breakdown is attached for your reference.` — only when an attachment is actually provided or planned by the user.
- `We recognize that this differs from the standard approach ...`
- `Please let us know what is needed from our side.`
- `Happy to discuss the implementation details.`

## Precision and cadence

- Keep acknowledgement paragraphs short.
- Use transitions such as `In the meantime`, `Since then`, and `In other words` when they clarify the connection.
- Use compact `(i)`, `(ii)`, and `(iii)` conditions when this is easier to read than several paragraphs.
- Repeat a key noun or defined term when precision benefits; synonyms are optional.
- Use concrete actions and relevant figures rather than vague summaries.
- Distinguish `we propose` from `we agree`, and tentative timing from a firm deadline.
- A softer invitation to advise may suit the recipient's own process; a direct confirmation request suits a decision that must be recorded.
- Keep mild notification framing when it fits the author's voice, but remove openings that merely delay the point.

## Calibration

Personal preferences require the user's instructions or approved samples. Recipient-specific politeness, transaction terminology, and one-off constraints are not universal style rules. Follow the learning procedure in SKILL.md for authorized personal updates.
